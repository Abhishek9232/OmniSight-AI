# Research Gap

The reviewed literature shows that online assessment research has addressed several individual aspects of examination integrity, including proctoring, assessment strategies, machine-learning-based analysis, and automated programming assessment. The reviewed work also indicates that AI-assisted monitoring can introduce the possibility of incorrect or misleading accusations when isolated automated observations are treated as definitive evidence.

A gap identified from the reviewed literature is the need for an academic examination prototype that brings together multiple sources of examination evidence within a single workflow. These sources can include examination activity, computer-vision-based monitoring events, behavioral information, and assessment performance data.

Another important gap is the interpretation of automated monitoring results. A monitoring event such as temporary face absence or detection of multiple faces should not, by itself, be treated as conclusive evidence of academic misconduct. There is therefore a need for an evidence-oriented approach in which relevant events are recorded, combined into meaningful features, and presented with their context so that the resulting integrity assessment can be interpreted by a human reviewer.

Based on the reviewed sources, OmniSight-AI addresses this gap by proposing an academic prototype that combines online examination management, basic AI-assisted monitoring, behavioral event logging, feature-based analysis, and an explainable integrity-risk assessment. The system is intended to support human review rather than automatically declare a student guilty of misconduct.

The reviewed literature supports the need for these individual capabilities and concerns, while the specific combination and implementation proposed for OmniSight-AI represents the project's design direction. This should be treated as a project-level research gap and proposed solution, not as a claim that no similar system exists.
